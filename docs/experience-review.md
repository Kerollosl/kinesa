# Kinesa experience review — 0.12

The companions restore the original illustrated designs: Leo’s fluffy orange mane and warm smile, Lou’s stocky calf silhouette and ivory horns, Juno’s expressive white head and layered brown feathers, and Mattie’s curls, lavender tunic and halo. Their familiar faces and soft dimensional shading remain in the painted artwork. The cast uses articulated illustrations, not procedural 3D meshes.

Painted heads, bodies, wings and arms stay on persistent joints. Continuous breathing and curiosity blend with pointer attention and greetings through damped springs; repeated taps retain each spring’s position and velocity. Blinks use painted eyelids matched to the original faces. Independent wing and halo movement adds life while keeping the original silhouettes recognizable.

The crew now stays busy through a behavior director: overlapping glances, wing stretches, foot shuffles and gentle sways keep the idle varied over longer sessions. Each character has a different rhythm and energy. Dance toggles a continuous groove; greetings, celebrations and wing hugs layer over it. Hops include anticipation and soft landings, with hearts or sparkles for deliberate reactions. The original head artwork stays attached throughout. Controls support keyboard use, and a long hold sends love while a scroll or canceled touch cancels the hold.

A shared animation scheduler updates visible companions without React rerenders on every frame. Scene pause preserves joints and effects while deliberate practice spins remain available. Hidden tabs and dialogs also suspend active practice-wheel transitions and defer their results until resume. Resume resets the wall-clock baseline to avoid a catch-up jump. Offscreen characters suspend. Reduced motion uses static poses while the game’s deliberate interactions remain available. Full-body scenes and face-focused portraits share the same self-hosted artwork. The implementation runs in React and SVG in the existing web app.

Validation: **21 unit tests, all 29 browser scenarios and the production build passed**. Motion checks cover spring continuity, bounded delayed frames, long-running idle behaviors, ongoing dance, action interruption, jump anticipation and landing, effect release, frame-rate agreement and portrait restraint. Browser checks exercise persistent parts, repeated greetings, pointer attention, long holds, scroll cancellation, exact pause/resume, dialog suspension, interrupted practice spins and dynamic motion preferences. They also verify that the original artwork and high-fives work with workers and WebGL disabled.

The frontend flow review includes 320px and 390px phone layouts, blocked local storage, complete four-player turns, interrupted and resumed play, empty and earned collection states, incorrect feedback, completed results and dialogs. Existing automated accessibility checks supplement the visual and interaction review; this pass does not establish performance or accessibility results on physical devices.

The app remains a local frontend preview. Matchmaking uses practice bots, friends use one device, and the question deck contains 12 samples. No production question database or remote multiplayer service was added. The publication target is the private `Kerollosl/kinesa` repository.
